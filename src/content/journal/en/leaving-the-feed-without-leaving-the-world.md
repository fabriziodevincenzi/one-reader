Social fatigue is not necessarily a rejection of relationship. Increasingly, it looks like a rejection of relationship built on continuous visibility.

A 2025 meta-analysis combined 107 empirical studies and 57,865 participants. It found that communication and technology overload were strongly associated with social media fatigue, which in turn was linked to reducing, suspending, or leaving platforms. [The research](https://www.rcommunicationr.org/index.php/rcr/article/view/240) also identifies privacy concerns, comparison, and fear of missing out as meaningful contributors.

The feed has no natural ending. Every thought can become content, every gesture a performance, every silence a possible sign. The question is not only how to use social media less, but what kind of connection we want to keep when we stop feeding the feed.

Letter-writing offers one answer. Recent reporting describes a renewed interest in pen-pal programs despite the decline of traditional mail. [The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/bc87e1b9c229665bafd368e19751d6ca) suggests that the desire to write to someone has not disappeared; it needs a less public and less accelerated form.

One Reader keeps connection without reproducing the feed: one person writes to one person, without an audience, score, or endless stream. Leaving the feed does not have to mean leaving the world. It can mean choosing more carefully where our words go.
