In [Rejsen ind i dig selv](https://www.20skridt.dk/rejsen-ind-i-dig-selv/), Ole Ditlev Nielsen suggests writing seven letters before turning seventy: to an old teacher, a friend we lost touch with, our younger self, or someone who is grieving.

The idea is not only about who receives the letter. It is also about what happens when we try to make something precise for another person. A letter asks us to discover what we actually mean.

But what if the person you need to write to is not someone from your past? What if it is a stranger?

A stranger does not know our history and has no fixed idea of who we should be. There is no shared past to explain and no role to maintain. That can make it easier to write without performing.

On social media, we often write to many people at once. We share something that can be understood quickly, recognised and perhaps answered in public. A letter works differently. It has one recipient. It may take time. It does not need to lead to another post or an endless conversation.

Perhaps that is why writing to someone we do not know can feel meaningful. Not because a stranger automatically understands us better, but because the letter gives us a place to be precise without having to be visible.
